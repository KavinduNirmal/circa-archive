# Organizations

People encounter Incidents for different reasons.

Some want to prevent another death. Some want to recover someone already missing. Others seek materials, knowledge, profit, or evidence for a belief they held before the first report arrived.

They do not stop having those reasons when they begin writing.

## Language

An organization gives names to the things it investigates. Those names reveal what it expects to find.

One may describe an occurrence as a disaster. Another may regard it as an opportunity. CIRCA files it as an [Incident](Anomalies.md).

The designations may refer to the same place, on the same night, with the same people inside. The accounts may disagree about almost everything that happened there.

Retain the designation used by the source alongside the archive reference. Translating the name should not erase the source's assumptions.

## Purposes and methods

For private organizations, [recoveries](Recoveries.md#commercial-value) can be goods for sale. Potions, artifacts, and resources with unusual effects give those organizations a material reason to enter an Incident and return to it.

That purpose may coexist with research or rescue. Record the objective of the particular expedition rather than assuming every organization seeks the same outcome.

Organizations also choose how much to disclose to [civilians](Civilians.md#secrecy), how to approach a rescue, and how their explorers protect their [identities](Anonymity.md). CIRCA's warning about identification does not amount to command of their operations.

## Personnel

CIRCA and certain organizations have [independent entities](Independent%20entities.md) among their staff and administration. Other members may know their nature or remain unaware of it.

Their cooperation or submission requires careful monitoring. An institutional position should not be treated as proof that the entity has lost its independent will or its connection to a Ghost Story.

Organizations may continue employing corrupted personnel who remain in ordinary reality as actors. CIRCA records these employment policies without endorsing them. Such personnel are not automatically independent Incident systems or severe return-seeking cases.

## Recognition

An organization is **recognized** when CIRCA has a file on it. Recognition establishes an archival reference. It is not an endorsement of its methods, its account of itself, or its claims about another organization.

A dossier gathers what has been written about it. A dossier may contain contradictions. Its existence does not grant any single account the last word.

## Claims

Several organizations may investigate the same Incident. Each brings its own methods and leaves its own records.

A **primary claim** identifies the account presented first in the Incident's file. It does not confer ownership of the phenomenon or prevent another organization from submitting a different account.

A contested file may remain contested.

An organization's account of its own conduct should be read beside accounts written by the people who encountered it.

## Codes and assessments

An organization's founding-carnage letter is part of its archival designation. It is fixed at promotion. An [Incident classification](Classifications.md) assesses the phenomenon described by a file.

Do not read an organization's code as a current assessment of every Incident it investigates. Cite the relevant Incident account.
