# Corruption

Departure from an [Incident](Anomalies.md) establishes that an explorer has left its observed space. It does not establish that the explorer has returned unchanged.

Continued exposure may produce changes that persist between visits. Repeated exploration and sustained use of anomalous [recoveries](Recoveries.md) are both relevant to the account.

Such changes are described here as **corruption**. The term concerns a person's relationship to ordinary reality. It is not a judgment of their character.

## Accumulation

A single expedition may have a recorded beginning and end. The explorer's exposure may extend across many expeditions, through different Incidents, and into the use of what they bring home.

Preserve that history. An effect first noticed after the latest visit need not have begun there.

The speed of corruption varies with the Incident explored and the duration of exposure. Some anomalous recoveries are difficult to maintain and can corrupt their users immediately. An uneventful first use does not establish a safe period for later use.

Consecutive participation can increase corruption exponentially, depending on classification. No fixed multiplier or safe repetition interval follows from that description.

Experience may improve an explorer's understanding of a particular rule. It does not establish protection from continued exposure.

## Belonging

A corrupted person may behave as an actor of the story they encountered while remaining in ordinary reality as an autonomous entity. Compulsive return to the source Incident is a severe case, not the normal outcome.

A person becomes an independent Incident only by establishing a new anchor and the system’s complete narrative flow. Acting as an entity outside the source story does not alone establish that development.

A missing explorer is not sufficient evidence of either. Nor should an account of return be treated as proof that no change has occurred.

Record what can be observed, what has been inferred, and who made the distinction. Do not supply a transformation merely to explain an absence.

## Suspected signs

Corruption cannot be identified reliably. Recurring dreams, anomalous familiarity, inconsistent memories, or unexplained knowledge may prompt investigation, but no single sign supplies a diagnosis.

CIRCA medical and investigative personnel use [Narrative Assimilation](Narrative%20Assimilation.md) as an illustrative progression. Its stages describe suspected changes; its reference percentages are not reliable measurements.

If signs appear, cease the current activity, isolate recoveries from further use, and take leave for mental recovery. Consult the counsellor provided by the explorer's organization. The [response notice](Narrative%20Assimilation.md#early-response-notice) describes what to record for that consultation.

Some organizations continue employing corrupted personnel. CIRCA records their policies without endorsing them. Continued employment and ongoing monitoring do not establish that the person has returned to their earlier state.

During active participation, pausing optional activity cannot revoke admission; the Incident's conclusion still governs departure.

## The limits of the account

No single duration of exposure establishes the same outcome for every explorer. An account of prolonged use should retain what was used, how often, and what changed afterward.

Claims of reversal require their own evidence. The cessation of an observed effect does not, by itself, establish a complete return to an earlier condition.
