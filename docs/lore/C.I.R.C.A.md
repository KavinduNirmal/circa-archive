# C.I.R.C.A.

Commission for Incident Review and Corrective Action.

This is the name presently used in correspondence. It is not known to be the first.

No founding document has been established. No surviving account identifies a founder. Records attributed to the Commission appear under different names, in different languages, among the remains of institutions that could not have corresponded with one another.

Some of those attributions are disputed. None has provided a satisfactory beginning.

Governments are aware of CIRCA. Some have supplied offices, personnel, or access to their archives. Such arrangements should not be mistaken for ownership. The Commission has outlasted the authorities whose seals appeared on its paperwork.

A government may cease to exist. Its outstanding files remain outstanding.

## The work

CIRCA receives accounts of occurrences that established understanding cannot adequately explain. It refers to these occurrences as [Incidents](Anomalies.md).

Other organizations investigate them. Individuals encounter them. Witnesses survive them, misunderstand them, or leave accounts from which neither survival nor understanding can be established.

The Commission files what reaches it.

A [record](Records.md) may contain an observation, an explanation, an instruction, or a lie. Its presence in the archive establishes that the account was filed. It does not establish that the account is true.

**CIRCA does not decide truth. It decides what is on file.**

The archive also uses a [recording arrangement](Witness%20sheets.md) made from class E Incidents. It preserves an explorer's recorded experience after death without requiring the explorer to return and report it.

The [Incident index](Archive%20index.md) begins at INC-07539. Ghost Stories precede the archive; the missing earlier records do not supply a date for their origin.

Explorers and archivists assess [outcomes](Outcomes.md#who-assesses-success) through the evidence available. The Commission's filing of that assessment does not certify an objectively successful exploration.

## Corrective action

The title has caused some confusion.

The Commission does not undertake to restore the world to an earlier condition. It does not promise containment, recovery, or rescue. An organization may submit an account of such an undertaking. CIRCA will review the account.

Within the archive, an attribution can be corrected. A missing reference can be supplied. Two reports thought to describe separate Incidents can be placed beside one another.

A casualty entered under the wrong name can be entered under the correct one.

This is a correction to the record. It does not reverse the casualty.

## Warnings

The Commission maintains a [warning against identification](Anonymity.md) during encounters. Ghost Stories observe those who enter them. Names, appearance, and other identifying details should be withheld where possible.

Organizations determine how they act on that warning. They also determine their approach to [civilian disclosure and care](Civilians.md). The Commission's receipt of an account does not establish its direction of the operation described.

CIRCA medical and investigative personnel also use [Narrative Assimilation](Narrative%20Assimilation.md) to describe suspected changes following exposure. It supplies a reference for assessment and response, not a reliable measurement of corruption or command of another organization's personnel.

The Commission does not actively pursue institutional secrecy. [Knowledgeable civilian groups](Civilians.md#survivor-groups) may exist, and organizations determine their own approach to them. The warning about personal identification during an encounter remains in effect.

## Personnel

The Commission's staff and administration include [entities](Independent%20entities.md) whose nature may be known or unknown to those working beside them. Their service does not establish how the Commission began or who ultimately directs it.

An actor’s institutional service does not make it an Incident system. CIRCA records organizations’ policies on continued employment of corrupted personnel without endorsing them.

Cooperation and submission remain matters requiring careful monitoring. An appointment is not a complete account of the person or presence holding it.

## Continuity of office

The Commission's present arrangements should not be taken as evidence of its original form. An archive need not have used that word to perform the work of one.

Names change. Offices move. The means of making a copy change.

The obligation to preserve the account remains.

Questions concerning the Commission's origin may be submitted with supporting documentation. An unsupported date, however frequently repeated, remains an unsupported date.

Until further evidence is received, the origin is unestablished.
