# Anonymity

Explorers observe Ghost Stories. Ghost Stories also observe explorers.

CIRCA warns against disclosing information by which an Incident could identify a person. Do not use actual names within the encounter. Conceal appearance where possible, and consider what other details may reveal who has entered.

**Do not assume that only the people accompanying you are listening.**

## Identifying details

A substitute name is only one precaution. A conversation can disclose relationships, affiliations, or details of a life outside the Incident without anyone formally introducing themselves.

Consider what is said to companions as well as what is said to a manifestation. An answer intended for a colleague may still be heard.

Covering a face does not establish that the explorer is unrecognized. An Incident's means of observation may not be understood.

## Organizational practice

Organizations decide how to approach anonymity. CIRCA records their methods and maintains the warning. Its [recording arrangement](Witness%20sheets.md) can support an account without placing the expedition under Commission command.

A method that appeared sufficient during one exploration does not establish protection during another. Record precautions and known disclosures alongside the outcome.

The warning does not establish that every Ghost Story has a human mind, or that all respond to recognition in the same way. Compare the [observed behavior](Phenomena.md#observed-behavior) rather than assuming awareness from a familiar appearance.

The Commission's lack of institutional secrecy does not withdraw this warning. [Civilian survivor groups](Civilians.md#survivor-groups) may know about Incidents; revealing an explorer's identity to a phenomenon is a separate matter.

## Other people

[Civilians](Civilians.md) may not know the warning. A rescue or interview inside an Incident should account for what could be revealed by either party.

Being asked for a name is not the only way to disclose one.
