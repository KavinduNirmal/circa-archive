# Continuities

A continuity is a connected sequence of recorded events. An Incident may have a principal continuity and divergent branches; an organization may likewise have several branches of its recorded story.

One account may describe an office closing after an evacuation. Another may describe work continuing there for years. Both may draw on earlier records. Neither can necessarily be made consistent with the other by correcting a date.

Preserving the branches allows their consequences to be followed.

## Adoption

An account is **adopted** when it becomes part of a named continuity. Filing alone does not establish adoption. A principal continuity identifies one accepted sequence, not universal truth about every account.

An adopted record may still contain a disputed interpretation. A well-supported independent record may remain outside the principal sequence.

Adoption does not alter what another witness reported or require every organization to accept the same conclusion.

## Independent accounts and branches

An independent record is not automatically a divergent continuity. A branch explicitly follows an alternative sequence and identifies its point of departure.

Records retain references, credit, and lineage. A death or a successor in one sequence does not establish the same event in another.

## Institutional continuity

The word also describes [CIRCA](C.I.R.C.A.md) itself: institutional persistence through changes of name, office, and surrounding authority.

That usage concerns the persistence of the work. A branch of recorded history concerns which accounts are followed together. Neither establishes that contradictory histories occupy separate universes.

The archive preserves the disagreement. It does not possess the evidence needed to explain every disagreement away.
