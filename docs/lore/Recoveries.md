# Recoveries

A recovery is an object or material brought out of an [Incident](Anomalies.md), or retrieved after its manifestation.

The term describes its history. It does not establish that it is useful, harmless, or understood.

Some objects are produced by an Incident. Others enter as ordinary belongings and leave altered. An object may also remain entirely ordinary while providing evidence of an extraordinary occurrence.

Record what is known of its origin. Do not supply one merely because it has been recovered.

## Working distinctions

**Residual recoveries** are objects left behind without an observed continuing connection to the manifestation. An absence of observed behavior should be stated with the duration and limits of observation.

**Active recoveries** retain anomalous behavior outside the original Incident. Describe that behavior and its conditions. “Active” does not mean the object behaves continuously.

**Anchor recoveries** concern movable anchors carried out of an encounter. Movement can change where access is encountered; it does not transfer ownership of the anchor, reproduce it, or establish that the source Incident has ended. Use this description where evidence supports a connection to the [anchor](Anchors.md), and retain any uncertainty.

These distinctions may require revision. An apparently residual object may become active under circumstances that were absent during examination.

## Removal

Removing an object and relocating an Incident are different outcomes.

An empty building does not establish that the phenomenon has been contained. An object that behaves elsewhere does not establish that the original site is inactive.

Where possible, compare observations from the recovery's new location with observations from the place it left.

Account separately for the object, the site, and the people who handled it.

## Commercial value

Some recoveries can do what ordinary objects cannot. A potion may produce an effect that would otherwise be impossible. An artifact may confer an unusual capability. Each claim still requires an account of what the recovery actually does and under what conditions.

Such effects give private organizations a reason to acquire and sell recovered objects and resources. An expedition may be funded for what can be brought back and sold, rather than for what can be learned.

Record the intended use where it is known. The price someone will pay does not establish safety, and a sale does not end the consequences of [continued exposure](Corruption.md).

## Continued use

A recovery may remain in use long after the expedition that produced it. Its immediate behavior is only part of the account.

Record the duration and frequency of use, and any persistent changes in the person using it. Continued use of anomalous objects or resources can expose that person to [corruption](Corruption.md) outside the original site.

A useful effect does not establish that its use is without consequence.

Some recoveries can corrupt a user immediately. An absence of gradual warning does not establish harmlessness. Where suspected changes occur, suspend use and follow the [early response notice](Narrative%20Assimilation.md#early-response-notice).

## Specialized claims

Some rare recoveries are said to avert an otherwise fatal experience. Others may protect possessions carried through [role-based entry](Entry.md#role-based-entry). Identify the particular item and its usage conditions before relying on either claim.

Only special recoveries permit participants to communicate with the outside world. Their identities and usage conditions must be established; ordinary equipment and witness-sheet playback do not supply that ability by default.

No such claim establishes a universal protection from death or loss. The conditions of one recovery do not identify those of another.
