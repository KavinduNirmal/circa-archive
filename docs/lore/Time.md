# Time

The time an explorer experiences within a Ghost Story need not match the time that passes outside it.

Most Incidents follow the same flow of time as ordinary reality. Some differ greatly or behave nonlinearly. The familiar rate should not be assumed before it has been established for the encounter.

## Two accounts of duration

An explorer may spend years trapped inside while only minutes pass outside. Another may remain absent for a month of external time and be considered dead by those awaiting their return.

Neither external absence nor an explorer's recollection supplies both durations. Record experienced time and externally observed time separately, including how each was established.

Where either remains unknown, retain that uncertainty. An estimate does not become a measurement because it is the only duration available.

## Survival and rescue

Time disparity is among the first conditions an explorer must consider. The demands of surviving a long confinement can arise before anyone outside has reason to suspect that such a confinement has occurred.

An external observer cannot determine years of internal experience from a few minutes of external absence alone. A plan that relies on rescuers arriving after an outside interval should account for what that interval could mean to the people inside.

CIRCA's receipt of a record does not establish that anyone could observe the explorer's condition while the encounter was in progress.

Outside communication requires a special recovery. A plan must establish that capability rather than assume a channel exists because an item was carried in.

## Interpreting an account

[Recorded perception](Recollection%20Projector.md#perception), internal clocks, recollection, and external observations may disagree. Preserve their sources rather than forcing them into a single chronology.

An earlier time relationship is evidence of an earlier encounter. It does not establish a dependable conversion for every later visit.

A long absence is relevant to a missing person's account. It does not, by itself, establish their death or the [outcome](Outcomes.md) of the encounter.
