# Civilians

An encounter does not require prior belief.

Ordinary people do not necessarily know that [Ghost Stories](Anomalies.md) exist. Some believe in particular stories. Others dismiss them until an experience becomes difficult to dismiss, and may continue doubting their own recollection afterward.

A person can survive an Incident without acquiring the vocabulary used to file it.

A civilian may not recognize admission and still be bound to the story’s conclusion. Once admitted, intentional explorers and accidental entrants are both [participants](Participation.md).

## After an encounter

A witness may need consultation, continued observation, or both. An interview taken immediately after departure need not be the last useful account.

Record what the person experienced, what they were told afterward, and what remained uncertain. Distinguish a later recollection from an explanation supplied by an investigator.

Uncertainty about what happened is not, by itself, evidence that the witness is still inside the Incident or has undergone [corruption](Corruption.md).

## Secrecy

CIRCA does not determine what an organization tells civilians.

One organization may disclose what it knows. Another may withhold its explanation. Their approaches can differ even when they are responding to the same occurrence.

Describe the approach taken and its consequences. A witness's account should not be presented as independent confirmation of an explanation they first heard from the organization interviewing them.

## Survivor groups

Some civilian survivors gather with others who know that Incidents exist. Their knowledge need not depend on employment by an investigative organization or formal participation in the archive.

CIRCA does not actively impose institutional anonymity or secrecy. An organization decides how to approach such groups, what to disclose, and what relationship to pursue.

A group's shared experience can provide useful accounts. Agreement within it does not establish that every member observed the same event or remained unaffected afterward.

## Rescue

An explorer entering to retrieve civilians must consider what those people know, what they can understand during the encounter, and what instructions they can follow.

A rescue plan that depends on prior familiarity with the Incident should account for the people who have none.

The need to give instructions must also be considered alongside [anonymity](Anonymity.md). An attempt to locate a missing person may disclose that person's name to more than the intended listener.

A rescue plan must also account for [time disparity](Time.md#survival-and-rescue). A short external absence may conceal a long period of confinement.

Returning a civilian from the space and accounting for what happens afterward are separate parts of the [exploration record](Explorations.md).
