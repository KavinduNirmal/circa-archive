# Anatomy of an Incident

An Incident is a supernatural system anchored to reality. An entity operating within that system is an actor. The two references should not be treated as interchangeable.

![Anatomy of an Incident: an anchor connects ordinary reality to a system containing nature, actors, and conditions.](Pasted%20image%2020261018211443.png)

## Parts of the system

| Part | Responsibility within the account |
| --- | --- |
| **Anchor or anchors** | Connect the Incident to ordinary reality and bear themes of the story within. |
| **Nature** | The environment, circumstances, and boundaries in which the story proceeds. |
| **Actor or actors** | Entities, forces, or participants driving events. |
| **Conditions** | Triggers, prohibitions, consequences, and completion rules. |

Nature and actor need not be separate. An environment may act, and some Incidents have no identifiable actor. Every Incident nevertheless requires an [anchor](Anchors.md) and a [beginning and conclusion](Narrative%20flow.md).

The [rules](Rules.md) govern how these parts operate together. A contradiction in an account is not permission to invent an arbitrary relationship among them.

## Identifying a system

Finding an actor is not sufficient to identify the whole Incident. Destroying one is not sufficient to establish that its nature has ended.

An [autonomous actor](Independent%20entities.md) may act beyond its source story without ending it. An actor that establishes a new anchor may become an independent Incident. That new system must itself have a beginning, conditions, and an ending.
