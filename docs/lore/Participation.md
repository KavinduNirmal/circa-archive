# Participation

An Incident defines the conditions under which a person becomes a participant. Admission need not coincide with crossing a visible boundary, and the person may not recognize when it occurs.

Once admitted, they cannot refuse participation. The path forward is the story's [conclusion](Narrative%20flow.md).

## Conditions of admission

Observation or awareness may suffice in some Incidents. Others require several conditions to be met, or an intentional declaration of participation.

A declaration can therefore be a condition of a particular system. This does not mean an Incident distinguishes a willing explorer from an accidental entrant after admission. Both are participants, subject to the same system.

Failure to recognize that participation has begun is one reason civilian casualties are high. A person cannot withdraw merely by explaining that they did not intend to enter.

## Application of rules

Once an operative condition is satisfied, accidental or unintentional satisfaction still counts. A rule requiring declared intent must itself be fulfilled; that does not create a general intent exemption for other conditions.

Different treatment of participants requires a governing [applicability rule](Rules.md#wording-and-application). Ignorance of that rule does not prevent its consequences.

## Observation from outside

Review through [The Recollection Projector](Recollection%20Projector.md) ordinarily remains outside the active Incident. The existence of a recording does not establish a live connection to the space it records.

## Exceptional participation

Where observation or knowledge is itself an admission condition, outside review can initiate participation. Such cases require careful monitoring.

Establish the particular condition. Do not assume every recording admits a viewer, or that every person outside a visible entrance is uninvolved.

## Shared nature

Participants in the same nature cannot experience different versions of it unless the nature itself is the actor.

They can still perceive it differently through hallucination, distortion, or manipulated memory. Contradictory [recorded perceptions](Recollection%20Projector.md#perception) do not by themselves establish separate versions of the setting.

## Communication and overlapping encounters

Communication with the outside world requires special [recoveries](Recoveries.md#specialized-claims). Ordinary carried equipment does not establish an outside channel.

A person cannot participate in multiple Incidents simultaneously except through [nested entry](Nested%20incidents.md), such as encountering Incident B's anchor while participating in Incident A.

## Repeated participation

A person may return for further runs. Consecutive participation is discouraged: corruption can increase exponentially in such circumstances, depending on the classification.

Familiarity with the beginning does not protect the person from the cost of repeating it.

## Independent actors

[Autonomous and emancipated actors](Independent%20entities.md) must be distinguished from the Incident systems they originate from. An actor's departure does not itself clear its source story.

## After departure

Recovered materials and corruption may retain consequences beyond the concluded run. Record those consequences separately from the active system's reach and from a later admission.
