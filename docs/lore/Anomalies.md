# On the Nature of Ghost Stories

Humanity has always sought to understand the universe through observation.

We establish laws, identify patterns, and construct explanations. What was once considered supernatural may become ordinary through understanding.

Yet there remain occurrences that resist explanation.

We have given them many names. Ghosts. Spirits. Hauntings. Urban legends. Anomalies.

[CIRCA](C.I.R.C.A.md) refers to them collectively as **Incidents**. Other organizations retain their own language. A different designation does not necessarily describe a different phenomenon.

The absence of an explanation does not imply the absence of [rules](Rules.md). An unreliable presentation has a governing cause, and unintelligibility does not permit arbitrary outcomes.

**Every Ghost Story has an anchor. Every manifestation has conditions. Every occurrence follows a pattern, whether we have recognized it or not.**

A mirror may invite a presence. A road may carry a passenger who never existed. A house may remember someone long after their death.

These occurrences appear impossible. Impossibility is a judgment made from the limits of our understanding.

## The account and the occurrence

Every ghost story has at least two versions: the story people tell, and the phenomenon described in the available records.

Neither should be confused with complete knowledge.

A warning may preserve a useful instruction after its explanation has been forgotten. A precise report may mistake coincidence for a condition. Several witnesses may repeat the same error because they heard the same story before entering.

An [Incident](Anatomy.md) is an anchored supernatural system; an actor is an entity operating within it. Every system has a beginning and a completion condition, even where the conclusion takes a long time to reach.

CIRCA preserves those accounts so that they can be compared. Filing them does not settle the disagreement.

## The observed pattern

An investigation may distinguish five elements:

1. **[Anchor](Anchors.md):** where the phenomenon connects to ordinary reality.
2. **Conditions:** the circumstances that permit a manifestation.
3. **Trigger:** the event that activates it once those circumstances are present.
4. **Manifestation:** what occurs while the phenomenon is active.
5. **[Outcome](Outcomes.md):** what ends or changes the encounter, distinguishing clearance, reset, suppression, and resolution.

These are questions to ask of an account. They are not five answers that every account can supply.

An open door may be an entrance without being an anchor. Midnight may be a condition without being a trigger. Leaving a room may end a witness's observations without ending the Incident.

An unexplained occurrence is not a reason to invent an explanation.

## Beyond the entrance

Almost all Incidents described in this body of work involve separate spaces that explorers physically enter. Some are instead entered [spiritually or during sleep](Entry.md#physical-and-nonphysical-entry); death there also kills the physical body. The approach may be an ordinary corridor, a roadside stop, or a room whose dimensions can be measured from outside.

What lies beyond need not fit inside those measurements.

Physical entry does not establish how the space exists. A room that resembles a childhood home is not evidence that the explorer has returned to it. An exit opening onto a familiar street is not, by itself, proof of return.

[Exploration](Explorations.md) records should distinguish what was encountered inside from what could be verified afterward.

The [time experienced inside](Time.md) may differ greatly from time outside. Most Incidents share the ordinary flow, but a long internal confinement can pass before external observers recognize that someone is missing.

Incidents are ordinarily bound to their anchors and isolated spaces. [Rare participation conditions](Participation.md) can nevertheless involve a person through observation or awareness alone.

## Disposition

Not every Ghost Story is malicious.

A deceased grandmother may continue setting the table each Sunday. A railway station may appear once every seven years without acknowledging anyone on its platform. A stranger may grant a request on the condition that the recipient never thanks them.

A reflection may gradually replace the person standing before it.

Such descriptions concern observed behavior. They do not establish motive. Something may be terrifying without causing harm, or reassuring until a condition changes.

Incidents established as non-hostile or harmless under known conditions are described as [class E](Classifications.md#class-e), regardless of their size or influence. Other classifications consider casualties and may also reflect the difficulty of survival or resolution.

A phenomenon may be [reactive, adaptive, intelligent, or indeterminate](Phenomena.md). Not every Ghost Story has a consciousness. Its behavior can nevertheless change between encounters, and an earlier classification may no longer describe the difficulty of returning.

Ghost Stories have occurred throughout history. The [archive index](Archive%20index.md) does not establish when they began, and their origin remains unknown.

Rare [autonomous actors](Independent%20entities.md) can leave their systems without ending them, and emancipated actors may persist after their source becomes inaccessible. An actor becomes an independent Incident only through a new anchor and a complete narrative flow.

The spaces of Ghost Stories form a [connected universe](Nested%20incidents.md), allowing rare encounters with other stories’ anchors or actors and extremely rare absorption of one Incident by another.

## What remains

Some Incidents leave nothing that can be recovered. Others leave objects, alter existing materials, or continue through something carried away. [Recovery](Recoveries.md) does not establish which has occurred.

Survival, understanding, and recovery are separate outcomes. None guarantees the others.

Leaving also need not end the effects of exposure. Repeated exploration and sustained use of anomalous recoveries may leave changes described as [corruption](Corruption.md).

The rules may exist independently of our knowledge of them.

**And the consequences of misunderstanding those rules may be irreversible.**
