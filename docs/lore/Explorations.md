# Explorations

An exploration is an attempt to learn what happens within an [Incident](Anomalies.md), under what conditions, and with what consequences.

The people making that attempt may be researchers, employees, volunteers, or people whose initial encounter was involuntary and who subsequently chose to investigate. Their reasons belong in the account. An employer's description of an assignment does not establish what the assignment became.

CIRCA receives the [records](Records.md). It does not direct the expedition.

## From a witness to an entrance

An investigation may begin when a member of an organization hears that someone has experienced a Ghost Story. That investigator may also contribute to the CIRCA archive. This does not make the inquiry a Commission assignment.

The witness may have entered without knowing such occurrences were possible. Fear, missing details, and the belief that they hallucinated can complicate their account. Preserve what they recall without treating every detail as established or dismissing the account because it is incomplete.

The explorer's task is to determine why the witness encountered the Incident, identify the connection to its [anchor](Anchors.md), and find whether entry can be made deliberately.

An interview supplies a starting account. Entering supplies further observations. Neither removes the need to compare the two.

## Before entry

A useful account distinguishes an instruction received beforehand from a rule established through observation.

“Do not answer the telephone” may be advice, superstition, deliberate misinformation, or the only instruction that matters. Record its source. If the telephone rings and no one answers, the consequence of answering remains unknown.

An instruction can be sensible to follow without having been proven. Preserve its exact wording: an actor may lie or misunderstand, and semantic differences may identify a lawful loophole. The explorer’s central task is to determine the [operative rules](Rules.md), not merely collect instructions.

Before entry, consider CIRCA's [warning about identification](Anonymity.md). The people entering are not the only observers of the encounter.

Where an expedition concerns [civilians](Civilians.md), record what they knew and what the explorers intended to tell them. A plan to rescue someone must account for more than the route by which the rescuers entered.

Where a [witness sheet](Witness%20sheets.md) is carried, record that fact. Its recording can preserve the encounter even if no explorer returns to describe it.

Consult the [entry account](Entry.md) for spiritual or sleep entry and any requirement to occupy a role. Record what was carried and whether the explorer's form or possessions changed on entry.

Compare any reported [internal and external time](Time.md). An explorer can experience a long confinement before enough time has passed outside for anyone to recognize the need for rescue.

## Assessing entry

Consult the [Incident classification](Classifications.md) and its supporting account. Most Incidents above C are not recommended for solo exploration. An uncategorized Incident offers no basis for assuming that entry alone is safe.

If an objective appears to require deaths or sacrifices, the explorer must weigh that cost against the consequences for society. Record the evidence for the requirement and the decision made. Survival, clearance, and the subsequent reset must be recorded as distinct results. A creative solution may change the ending, but an admitted participant cannot withdraw merely by deciding to stop.

Review earlier resets as well as earlier entries. [Adaptive or intelligent phenomena](Phenomena.md) may no longer behave as the filed account describes, particularly after repeated investigations or changed reset conditions.

Check for [participation conditions](Participation.md) that make observation or awareness sufficient for an encounter. Ordinary playback and review should not be assumed to create participation, but the rare exceptions matter to planning.

## Inside

Describe what the explorer could observe. Preserve the order of events and any uncertainty about it.

A watch stopping is an observation. Time stopping is an explanation. Record the explorer’s experienced duration separately from elapsed time established outside the Incident.

If two members remember different routes through the same corridor, retain both accounts. Their perceptions may differ without their occupying different versions of the same nature. Different versions are possible only where nature itself is the actor. Agreement reached after comparing accounts should not be presented as agreement at the time.

Outside communication requires a special recovery. Record the item and its established conditions rather than assuming ordinary radios, phones, or the recording arrangement supply a channel.

Different treatment of two participants requires an applicability condition. Distinguish that rule from an actor’s statement about it, and a contradiction between accounts from a genuine contradiction within the system.

## Outcomes

An explorer may survive without discovering a single rule. Another may correctly identify a condition and die before leaving.

An account should distinguish [survival, escape, clearance, reset, suppression, and resolution](Outcomes.md), alongside what was learned, what was recovered, and whether the assignment's stated objective was achieved.

Survival does not by itself establish escape. Clearance does not establish survival. Clearance ends the current run; a reset prepares the stage for another beginning, possibly after a delay. An observed reset should be recorded separately from the evidence for the conclusion.

The explorer and archivist assess the outcome through the available evidence. CIRCA does not independently certify success. Where accounts conflict, preserve the disagreement and exercise caution when using them to plan another entry.

A recovered witness-sheet recording establishes a source for reviewing the encounter. It does not establish survival of the carrier. Record the return of a person, recovery of a body, and recovery of recorded experience separately.

Where an objective changes during the attempt, preserve the original objective as well. “Return with the missing personnel” and “return” are different assignments.

## Continued exposure

Consecutive participation is discouraged because corruption can increase exponentially, depending on classification. Repeated returns are not evidence that an explorer has become immune to the Incident. Familiarity may conceal changes that a first account would have recorded.

Preserve the history of entry and any sustained use of [recovered objects or materials](Recoveries.md). Where changes persist after departure, consult the accounts concerning [corruption](Corruption.md).

Where suspected signs of corruption appear, stop optional exposure and seek assessment at the first available opportunity. This does not permit withdrawal from a story already in progress; participation remains bound to its conclusion. Isolate recoveries from continued use, take leave for mental recovery, and consult the organization's counsellor. Follow the [Narrative Assimilation response notice](Narrative%20Assimilation.md#early-response-notice); do not use a stage percentage as a diagnosis.
