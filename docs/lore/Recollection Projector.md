# The Recollection Projector

**Archive designation:** INC-E-10743

**Classification:** [E](Classifications.md#class-e)

**Form:** Overhead projector

A prepared [witness sheet](Witness%20sheets.md) is put through the Recollection Projector. The projector presents the carrier's recorded vision with sound.

This allows an explorer's experience to be reviewed after the encounter, including when the explorer has died.

## Perception

The projector faithfully reproduces the carrier's perceptions. Those can include hallucinations, manipulated memories, and sensory distortions caused by the phenomenon.

It does not supply observations the carrier never made.

If one carrier perceived a child and another perceived an older woman, both projections may faithfully reproduce their respective experiences. Neither projection alone establishes which figure existed independently of those perceptions.

The archivist must investigate the contradiction, including possible hallucination or influence from the Incident’s nature. Different perceptions do not establish different versions of the same nature; that exception requires the nature itself to be the actor. The fidelity of playback does not settle its cause.

## Participation

Ordinary playback is review from outside the active Incident. It does not, by itself, constitute entry into the recorded Ghost Story.

Rare phenomena can make observation or awareness an entry condition. Such [participation exceptions](Participation.md#exceptional-participation) require careful monitoring; the usual distinction between playback and participation should not be treated as universal protection.

## Review

A projection supplies an account from the carrier's position. It does not supply the explanations they lacked while inside.

Distinguish what can be observed in the projection from conclusions reached during review. If several analysts describe the same projection, their descriptions remain dependent on the same recorded experience.

A person visible in a recording has not thereby been recovered. A voice heard during playback has not thereby answered a new question.
