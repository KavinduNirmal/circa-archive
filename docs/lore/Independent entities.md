# Independent entities

An Incident is an anchored supernatural system. An actor is an entity operating within it.

An actor may act outside its original story without becoming another Incident. Its agency and the system's continued existence are separate matters.

## Entities brought out

An **autonomous actor** may leave its Incident without ending it, while retaining a connection to the source story.

An **emancipated actor** may continue existing after the original Incident becomes inaccessible. Inaccessibility does not by itself establish that the source was destroyed or that every relationship to it is understood.

An actor need not carry an entire surrounding nature into ordinary reality. Such absence does not make it a complete, unanchored Incident.

## New Incidents

An actor may establish a new anchor and become an independent Incident. The new system must have its own [beginning, conditions, and conclusion](Narrative%20flow.md).

Leaving the original space, surviving its inaccessibility, and establishing a new anchored system are different developments. Cite the evidence for the development claimed.

## Institutional service

CIRCA and certain organizations include entities among their staff and administration. Colleagues may know their nature or remain unaware of it.

Some organizations also continue employing corrupted personnel who behave as actors of the stories that affected them. CIRCA records these policies without endorsing them.

Employment does not establish an independent Incident or identify an anchor.

## Cooperation and submission

Cooperation or submission requires careful monitoring. Preserve the arrangement, the observed conduct, and the evidence about any continuing story connection.

Neither service nor apparent compliance establishes permanent allegiance, harmlessness, or complete control.
