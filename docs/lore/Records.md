# Records

A record is an account preserved by [CIRCA](C.I.R.C.A.md).

An Incident is what the account concerns. The record is what can be read.

This distinction is necessary. An occurrence may leave several incompatible accounts. A single account may describe occurrences that its author has mistakenly joined together.

## Kinds of account

An **INCIDENT** record preserves the original telling. It may describe a witness’s experience, a recollection, or first contact, and may state or hint at governing conditions. The narrator need not understand them. An explanation supplied later should not be presented as knowledge they possessed then.

A **DISCOVERY** record describes an organization's contact with the Incident: how it came to attention, what was encountered, and what the organization initially understood.

An **EXPLORATION** record follows the investigation further. Interviews, recovery accounts, containment attempts, and later visits may contribute to it. Each supplies evidence from a particular position.

A **RUMOR** preserves an unverified claim. It may contain a truth absent from better-supported accounts. It may also be an invention. Its value cannot be settled by its label alone.

## Recorded experience

An account may be based on a [witness sheet](Witness%20sheets.md) rather than a surviving narrator. The prepared paper preserves an explorer's vision; projection makes it available for review with sound, even after the explorer's death.

Identify the recording as the source and distinguish it from the written interpretation. A report prepared by an analyst after playback is not testimony composed by the dead explorer.

The recording preserves the encounter from its carrier's position. It does not explain what the carrier could not understand.

A faithful recording can preserve a hallucination or sensory distortion. [The projector](Recollection%20Projector.md#perception) reproduces perception, not an independent view of reality. Contradictory projections require investigation rather than an assumption that one sheet failed.

Review of a recording ordinarily differs from participation in an active Incident. Record any known [exceptional entry condition](Participation.md#exceptional-participation) rather than assuming all review is either exposing or protective.

## Statements about rules

A verbatim instruction, a notice supplied by nature, and an explorer's inference are different sources. Preserve the wording and attribution. An actor can lie about a rule or fail to know one itself.

Contradictory accounts may remain on file. That does not establish genuinely contradictory laws in the phenomenon. [Rules](Rules.md#contradiction) require a coherent system, with true contradictions confined to multiple-primary-actor cases.

## References

An Incident has its own designation, separate from the records filed about it. The [archive index](Archive%20index.md) begins at INC-07539; missing earlier files do not establish that earlier Incidents did not exist. An organization's name for the Incident is another reference to it, not ownership of the occurrence.

Cite the account on which a conclusion depends. Preserve the earlier reference when correcting an attribution. A later reader must be able to distinguish a new observation from a repeated one.

Ten accounts quoting the same witness do not establish ten witnesses.

## Filing

**Filed** means preserved in the archive. **Adopted** means accepted into a named [continuity](Continuities.md), whether an Incident’s principal sequence or an organization’s recorded story.

Neither word means proven.

A correction should make the history of a conclusion easier to follow. It should not make that history disappear.
