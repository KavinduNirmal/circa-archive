# Rules

Every Ghost Story has rules governing its [nature and actors](Phenomena.md). Failure to understand those rules does not establish their absence.

An incomprehensible occurrence still follows a basis for occurring. Determining that basis is an explorer's central task.

## What is said and what applies

An actor may state a rule verbatim. The nature may provide a notice. An explorer may infer a condition from what happened.

Preserve those sources separately. An actor can lie about the rules, or be unaware of a rule that governs its own conduct. A notice requires interpretation. An inference requires evidence.

An unreliable account of the story must also have a governing cause. Unreliability is something to investigate, not an explanation for any result whatsoever.

## Wording and application

Exact wording matters. A loophole can arise from the meaning of a condition and the way it is satisfied.

Where a rule affects participants differently, another governing rule must determine that difference. The same condition cannot simply apply one way to one participant and another way to someone else without a basis in the system.

A condition can be satisfied unintentionally. Where the rule is fulfilled, the accidental method does not invalidate the result. Conversely, not knowing a prohibition does not prevent its consequences.

## Intelligence and its limits

Intelligence does not grant unrestricted authority over the rules.

| Layer | Ordinary limit of intelligent alteration |
| --- | --- |
| **Participation rules** | May change the conditions governing admission or participation. |
| **Completion requirements** | May change what must be satisfied to reach the conclusion. |
| **Underlying story and ending** | Cannot ordinarily be rewritten merely because an actor dislikes the result. |
| **Actor behavior and limitations** | Continue to constrain what the actor can do. |

Exceptions occur among higher classifications. The particular exception requires its own account; the label alone does not establish an unlimited ability to rewrite the system.

An actor may recognize exploitation and still lack permission to prevent it. A talk-show actor might find repeated correct answers suspicious or entertaining while remaining unable to change the questions.

## Loopholes and conclusions

A permitted loophole uses the system's conditions to reach an available [ending](Narrative%20flow.md). It is distinct from an actor replacing that ending at will.

Record the wording, the action that satisfied it, and the resulting conclusion. Do not assume that the explanation offered by an actor is the only possible interpretation of the operative rule.

## Contradiction

Conflicting statements are not necessarily conflicting rules. A lie, an incomplete inference, or an unrecognized applicability condition may explain the disagreement.

Truly contradictory operative rules occur only in systems with multiple primary actors. Outside that case, contradiction is incompatible with the basis on which the system manifests.

The archive may preserve incompatible accounts while the actual system remains coherent. Filing both does not make both explanations its laws.

## Anchors

The rules also govern the [anchor](Anchors.md). Its theme, behavior, and relationship to the story belong to the same system.

An apparent change should therefore be examined through conditions, evidence, and the limits of the actor. Surprise alone does not establish that the rules were discarded.
