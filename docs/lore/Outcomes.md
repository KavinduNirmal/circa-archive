# Outcomes

An account should state what occurred before calling an exploration successful.

Contact, survival, return, and completion answer different questions. An Incident may remain available for further encounters after the current story concludes.

## Terms in an account

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Encounter** | Contact with a phenomenon, whether intended or accidental. |
| **Exploration** | A deliberate attempt to investigate its nature. |
| **Survival** | Leaving an encounter alive. This does not by itself establish return to ordinary reality or freedom from harm. |
| **Escape** | Crossing back into ordinary reality. Once participation begins, this cannot be treated as a refusal to continue; the system's conclusion must be accounted for. |
| **Clearance** | Reaching the story's conclusion under its completion conditions, including a permitted alternative ending. Clearance does not guarantee survival. |
| **Reset** | Returning the stage to its starting operational state for another run. This ordinarily follows clearance, immediately or after a delay. |
| **Suppression** | Temporarily preventing further manifestations. |
| **Resolution** | Eliminating or neutralizing an Incident's immediate consequences. |

These terms describe different results. Clearing the present run does not establish permanent destruction of the Incident.

## Clearance and reset

A [story concludes](Narrative%20flow.md), then its stage can reset. Once participants have left and entry conditions are met, another run begins from the beginning. A cooldown may delay reappearance.

An explorer may achieve a creative or alternative ending by outsmarting an actor. Record why that result satisfies a conclusion rather than describing it as an unconditional withdrawal from participation.

A reset establishes a change of operational state. Its observation should still be distinguished from evidence about who survived or which conclusion was reached. Adaptive behavior may change a later run.

## Who assesses success

The explorer states the outcome they believe they achieved. The archivist assesses that claim through the evidence and [exploration records](Explorations.md). CIRCA does not supply a universal verdict of successful exploration.

Keep the stated assignment, the explorer's account, and the archivist's assessment distinct. Completing an assignment may differ from clearing the story.

Where evidence contradicts the claimed result, preserve the contradiction and account for it in further plans.
