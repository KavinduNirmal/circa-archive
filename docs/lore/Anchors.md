# Anchors

Every [Incident](Anatomy.md) has an anchor. Some have several.

An anchor connects the supernatural system to reality. It may be an object, a location, or a living person. Its themes reflect the story within and can offer a glimpse of that story's nature or conclusion.

A mirror may show a corridor that does not exist behind the wall. The mirror is a surface of manifestation. If someone passes through it, it is also an entrance. Neither observation alone proves that it is the anchor.

## Entry

The method of entering an Incident is bound to its anchor and [participation conditions](Participation.md). An entrance may appear elsewhere, but access remains part of the system's conditions.

Entry may be physical, spiritual, or [through sleep](Entry.md). A living anchor or an awareness condition need not resemble a doorway.

Knowing where a witness entered is insufficient. Establish which conditions admitted them, including conditions they may not have recognized.

## Movement and ownership

Anchors cannot change ownership or reproduce. A movable anchor can change location; a location anchor remains fixed.

Moving, carrying, or encountering an anchor does not establish that its ownership or connection has changed. A person carrying one has not thereby become the owner of its Incident.

An actor may establish a new anchor and become an [independent Incident](Independent%20entities.md#new-incidents). This does not mean the source anchor reproduced or was transferred to the new system.

## Reach beyond the anchor

Incidents ordinarily operate within their rules and boundaries. Autonomous actors, recovered materials, and continuing corruption require distinct accounts of what acts beyond those boundaries.

An anchor can also be found within another [Incident](Nested%20incidents.md). Its location inside a story does not make it part of the same system by default.

The [rules](Rules.md#anchors) govern the anchor as part of the same system. Its apparent behavior and the story’s conditions must be examined together.

## Establishing the connection

Compare observations before and after a movable object or person changes location. Preserve which manifestations followed, which remained, and which conditions could not be reproduced.

An unsuccessful repetition does not establish that the Incident ended. A different result may concern entry conditions, a mistaken anchor identification, or another connected system.

## Changes of anchor

An apparent change of anchor must be distinguished from movement of the same anchor, the discovery of another existing anchor, or a mistaken earlier identification. Do not assume that an Incident can freely replace its anchor or that anchors can replicate.

Keep the earlier observations and record what supports the current identification. Suspected anchor movements belong in the [recovery account](Recoveries.md) where an item was carried out.
